🔤 4C — Grammar

Gerund Phrases Vs. Participle Phrases

မှတ်ရန်: Gerund = -ing form that works as a noun. Participle = verb form that can work as an adjective or a verb.
A. Study the examples of Gerund and Participle and complete the table.
DifferenceGerundParticiple
DefinitionA gerund is a progressive form (-ing) of a verb that works as a noun in a sentence.A participle is a verb form that works either as an adjective or as a verb in a sentence.
Types1. The subject
2. The object of a verb
3. The object of a preposition
4. The object of a possessive pronoun
5. The subject complement		1. Present participle
2. Past participle
FunctionsA gerund functions only as a noun.A participle can function either as an adjective or a verb.
Positions 1. Before the main verb (as the subject)
2. After an action verb (transitive) — object of a verb
3. After a linking verb — subject complement
4. After a preposition — object of a preposition
5. After a possessive adjective — object of a possessive pronoun 		1. Just before a noun
2. Just after a noun
3. After a linking verb (main verb)
Examples: Gardening is fun. / I love travelling. → Gerund (noun).
The boy playing football in the rain is my nephew. / We are living in a developing country. → Participle.
